## 2. Choose mapping

1. Review the model-catalog hits before choosing a mapping. `design_init` runs before this stage. It derives queries from the molecule's rings, substituent junctions, functional groups with neighbouring atoms, and aliphatic chains. It queries the prepared indexes (models, FF300, Bible) and ranks the hits by similarity to the target; prefer hits with support `atoms` as mapping precedents. Add a query with `design_query` when a motif is missing. Use `design_match` to find exact fragment precedents from both the model catalog and the prepared Bible. Open relevant references by ID with `design_open` to see the available bead types, atom mappings, and source locators. Give each opened reference a reason with `design_annotate`. `design_bead` also records when it uses a reference. The pipeline saves queries, model hits, annotations, and identity matches in `decisions/analogue_search.json`. Finding the same constitution or a matching canonical tautomer in either index sets `reference_exposed`.
2. Consult relevant prepared Bible entries, SI text, and source mappings. Cite the exact file and page, table, molecule section, or Bible row for each transferred decision. If the available text and mapping data leave a figure-dependent detail unresolved, record that limitation with `design_note`.
3. **Partition first, type second.** Cut the molecule into fragments before choosing any bead type (Souza 2021 SI section C1, p. 36; [MEV:souza-si:C:steps]):
   - A bead is a fragment of its heavy atoms. 4-1, 3-1 and 2-1 fragments default to R, S and T beads. Aromatic ring fragments are T, or S with a substituent; aliphatic rings use S; a fully branched fragment takes a smaller bead (neopentane, 5 atoms, is R). Keep the whole molecule within ±1 heavy atom per 10 of what its beads represent [MEV:souza-si:C1:default-sizes, MEV:souza-si:C1:branched].
   - Do not divide a chemical group (amide, carboxylate, ester) between beads [MEV:souza-si:C1:keep-groups], and do not isolate it either: a functional group goes into a 2–4-atom fragment with its neighbouring carbons (methoxy C–O, hydroxyethyl C–C–OH, phenol C–OH with its ring CH). A lone heteroatom or a lone carbon is not a bead; the only published exceptions are those the build's bead-size check accepts (for example the S of thiophene).
   - Never choose a cut to make a bead match a precedent's type. Precedents guide where to cut only when the same atoms form the same bead there; the type is assigned afterwards (stage 3).
   - `build` flags every bead whose size does not fit its heavy atoms (`bead_size_matches_heavy_atoms`). Change the mapping, or give the bead a `size_exception` (`design_bead`) that cites a published model with the same atoms in a bead of that size.
4. Weigh the supplied mappings (at most three). Consider functional groups, symmetry, charge localization, branching, ring shape, resolution, overall volume and motif junctions.
5. Work in full-molecule context. Local decomposition is allowed; inspect each junction and conjugated group. Do not blindly concatenate fragment topologies.
   - **Choosing among precedents.** Prefer a partition in which every bead holds exactly the fragment of a documented bead (an exact precedent, so each bead can be typed at rung 1). Among such partitions, or when none exists, start from the closest context, in this order: (a) the same ring system with the same fusion, as a published model (for example the force-field nucleobases for purines and pyrimidines, CAFF for xanthines); (b) the same fused ring pair with other substituents (BZIM, INDO, QUIN); (c) the isolated ring (IMID, PYMI, PYRI). Take its bead split and hinge layout first. Moving down the order, or departing from that precedent's split, is allowed when chemistry or geometry justifies it (a substituent, tautomer or protonation change, or a virtual site that misses the 0.05 nm limit); state the reason in the bead rationale and in the rejection reason of the closer precedent. Different defensible mappings are acceptable, as between chemists; an unexplained departure from a closer precedent is not.
   - **Junctions between a rigid group and a flexible part** (for example the glycosidic N9–C1′ of a nucleoside): keep each atom in a bead of its own part. Do not put an atom of a flexible ring or chain into a rigid-group bead. Otherwise several flexible beads connect to the rigid group, stage 4 bridges each to it, and the flexible part is over-restrained.
6. The pipeline projects the bead memberships of the most similar reference models onto the molecule and puts the resulting partitions into the mapping task (`design_propose` repeats this), each with its SASA against the atomistic ensemble (a second criterion after exact precedents, never the only one); between equally exact partitions, rings whose beads share atoms come before rings cut into unshared beads. The research step records the three best-ranked complete partitions within the mapping rules as P1-P3 and chooses P1; the mapping step confirms P1 or chooses P2 or P3 with `design_choose` and a reason. No agent records a partition of its own: the harness supplies the candidates, already checked against the mapping rules (no lone-atom bead, no bead larger than an R bead, no divided chemical group), each bead with its source and each partition with its SASA. Only when the pipeline finds no complete partition within the rules does the mapping agent record partitions of its own with `design_candidate`, which applies the same rules. Shared-atom allocation weights are part of the supplied partitions. `build` draws the mapping over the indexed molecule.
7. Every bead must contain at least one atom; the build cannot hold a bead with no atoms (for example the central TC4 of the Bible glucose). When a documented model has one, transfer the other beads and record the omission as a deviation.
8. Check connected groups, atom coverage and intentional overlap. Allocation weights sum to one across beads for each AA atom; normalize within each bead to compute its center of geometry. Include hydrogens per the source convention. Mapping weights do not define bead masses.
